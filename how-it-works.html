The Process

Our Five-Step Mortgage Assistance Process

The same structured process is followed with every homeowner so that nothing is left to guesswork. You may stop at any point, and there is never an obligation to proceed.

  1. Submit Your Information

    You begin by completing the eligibility or application form with basic information about your mortgage and your household finances. Only the details needed to understand your situation are requested, and the more accurately you complete the form, the more useful the initial review will be.

  2. Initial Review

    Our team reviews the information you have provided to understand your circumstances and to identify what additional information may be needed. We explain what we can and cannot help with, and what the next steps are likely to involve.

  3. Mortgage Information and Authorization

    Where appropriate, you provide a recent mortgage statement and any required authorisation documents. Written authorisation is normally necessary before any company can discuss account-specific information with your mortgage servicer, so this step is about making sure the paperwork is in place if servicer communication is needed.

  4. Financial Information Review

    You may be asked for information about your income, household expenses, hardship and supporting documents. Accurate, updated information matters because servicers base their review on your current circumstances — incomplete or out-of-date figures are a common reason for delay or for a package being returned.

  5. Submission and Servicer Review

    If an application is prepared and submitted, the mortgage servicer and/or the investor evaluates it under the applicable requirements. The servicer makes the decision. Review periods vary and depend on the servicer, the investor, the completeness of the documentation and the complexity of the case. Additional documents may be requested during the review, approval is not guaranteed, and a trial payment plan or other requirements may apply depending on the option offered.

No Two Mortgages Are Alike

Every Mortgage Situation Is Different

The process described above is the framework we follow, but the options that may actually be available to you depend on several factors. These include the type of loan you have, the guidelines set by the investor who owns the loan, your own financial circumstances, how far behind the mortgage is, and whether there is any foreclosure timeline already running.

Because of that, two homeowners in apparently similar situations can be eligible for very different options, or none at all. A loan backed by one investor may allow a particular modification, while a differently owned loan may not. A homeowner who is two payments behind may have more choices than one whose account has already moved further along the process.

We set out this context honestly because it shapes what we can realistically help with. Our role is to help you understand your situation, prepare accurate information, and identify what is worth discussing with your servicer — not to promise a particular result. To see how these options are grouped, visit Our Services, and see our FAQs for the questions homeowners ask most often.

Preparing

What You Will Need to Prepare

Having common documents to hand helps the review move forward. Exact requirements depend on your servicer, so treat this list as a practical starting point.

A Recent Mortgage Statement

Your most recent statement shows the loan balance, the current status and the amount outstanding, which helps confirm where the account stands.

Proof of Income

Recent pay statements, benefit letters or other evidence of income are commonly requested so your household income can be verified.

Household Expense Figures

A simple summary of your regular monthly expenses helps show what you can realistically afford and supports the review.

A Written Hardship Explanation

A short letter describing what changed financially — such as a job loss, illness or reduced income — helps explain your circumstances.

Any Notices Received

If you have received letters or notices from your servicer, keep them together, as key dates on those documents can affect the options available.

Required documents and the review process depend on your mortgage servicer and/or investor. MMCV cannot guarantee that an application will be approved or that any particular outcome will follow.
