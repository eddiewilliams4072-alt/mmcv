Each area below explains what the assistance generally involves and what it does not do. The option that may apply to you, if any, is decided by your mortgage servicer and/or investor.

01

Mortgage Modification Assistance

A mortgage modification is a change to one or more terms of an existing mortgage loan. It is not a new loan and it is not a form of direct financial assistance. A modification is granted only when the mortgage servicer and, in many cases, the investor who owns the loan agree to change the original terms of the agreement.

Depending on the loan and the applicable guidelines, a modification may change the interest rate, extend the loan term or duration, change how the unpaid principal balance or the past-due amount is treated, or adjust the monthly payment amount. Sometimes several of these changes are combined; in other cases only one term changes, and the overall payment may stay the same or even increase.

  • A change to the interest rate.
  • An extension of the loan term or duration.
  • A different treatment of the unpaid principal balance.
  • A different treatment of the past-due (arrears) amount.
  • An adjustment to the monthly payment amount or to other loan terms.

Whether a modification is approved, and on what terms, is decided by the mortgage servicer and/or investor under their own requirements. A reduction in the monthly payment is not guaranteed and no outcome should be assumed before a decision is issued. Because those requirements are based on your current circumstances, updated financial documents are usually requested. For a more detailed walkthrough, read our Mortgage Modification Guide.

02

Mortgage Delinquency Assistance

Falling several payments behind on a mortgage is stressful, and the outstanding amount can grow quickly once late charges and other fees are added. The first step is to understand exactly where the account stands and what is owed.

We help homeowners build a clear picture of the delinquency and review the options that may apply to their loan. Reinstatement — paying the entire past-due amount in a single sum — is one route, but it is not the only one, and for many households it is not realistic. Repayment plans, forbearance and modifications can sometimes bring an account back to a current status, though each has its own requirements and consequences.

  • Understanding the outstanding delinquency and the total amount due, including any fees.
  • Reviewing the mortgage account and its current status on your most recent statement.
  • Identifying the assistance programmes that may apply to your loan type and servicer.
  • Understanding how a repayment plan works and how it differs from a modification or forbearance.
  • Reviewing the other loss mitigation options your servicer may offer.

03

Foreclosure Assistance

If you have received a notice of default, a notice of intent to foreclose, or any other foreclosure-related document, time matters. The deadlines set out in those documents can affect the options still available to you, so it is important to act promptly rather than wait and hope the situation resolves itself.

We help homeowners understand the documents they have received and the loss mitigation options that may remain open at this stage. Because foreclosure rules, timelines and homeowner protections vary considerably by state, we also encourage every homeowner facing foreclosure to obtain independent legal advice from a qualified attorney licensed in their state.

  • Reviewing the current foreclosure status and any notice you have received.
  • Understanding the important dates and deadlines stated in those documents.
  • Communicating with your mortgage servicer about loss mitigation options.
  • Exploring the options that may still be available at this stage of the process.
  • Obtaining independent legal advice from a qualified attorney in your state.

Important: MMCV cannot guarantee that a foreclosure will be stopped, delayed or prevented, and our guidance is not a substitute for legal advice. Please see our Foreclosure Assistance Information for further context.

04

Financial Hardship Documentation

Most mortgage assistance requests are reviewed on the strength of the documents submitted with them. We help homeowners organise the information a servicer commonly asks for, so that the package is complete and consistent before it is sent.

  • A written hardship letter explaining the circumstances.
  • Income documentation and recent pay statements.
  • Bank statements, when the servicer requests them.
  • Household expense information.
  • Recent mortgage statements.
  • Any other documents the servicer requires.

Accurate, complete and up-to-date information is important. Servicers commonly return or delay incomplete packages, which can slow down a review and, in some cases, allow important deadlines to pass. Where your circumstances change — a new job, a change in household income, or added expenses — it is better to update the package than to submit outdated figures.

05

Mortgage Servicer Communication

When the homeowner has given the appropriate written authorisation, MMCV may assist with communication and coordination with the mortgage servicer about account-specific information. This can help keep correspondence organised and reduce the chance that a request is misunderstood or a deadline is missed.

In most cases a servicer will not discuss an account with a third party until it has received a valid written authorisation signed by the homeowner, so that authorisation is normally required before any account-specific conversation can take place. It is equally important to know that you may always contact your servicer directly and independently. You do not need MMCV, or any other company, to speak with your own servicer.

Understanding Available Assistance Options

The options that may be available depend on your eligibility, the type of loan you have, and the programmes offered by your servicer, the investor, or a government programme. We help you understand what each option generally involves so that you can make informed decisions and discuss them with your servicer.

  • Mortgage modification.
  • Repayment plans.
  • Forbearance.
  • Partial claim or other applicable loss mitigation options.
  • Reinstatement assistance.
  • Other programmes offered by the servicer, investor or government.

We do not decide which options you qualify for, and eligibility rules differ from one loan and servicer to another. We do not claim that any particular government programme is available to you, and we do not promise universal eligibility.
